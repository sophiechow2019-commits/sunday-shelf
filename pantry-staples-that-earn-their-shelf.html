Pantry Staples That Earn Their Shelf Space

You don't need forty ingredients. You need about fifteen that combine well, plus three sauces you can make from most of them.

Pantry lists usually turn into a shopping spree at a specialty grocer, which defeats the purpose. This one is deliberately short: everything on it is available at a regular supermarket, and every item is used in more than one of the sauces below. If an ingredient only works in one recipe, it didn't make the list — that's the whole filter.

The core fifteen

  • Rice
  • Canned beans (black + chickpea)
  • Canned crushed tomatoes
  • Olive oil
  • Soy sauce
  • Rice vinegar
  • Garlic
  • Onions
  • Ground cumin
  • Smoked paprika
  • Chili flakes
  • Tahini or peanut butter
  • Honey
  • Dijon mustard
  • Good salt

Three sauces, most of the list

These three sauces are why the pantry list stays short. Learn them once and they cover the majority of weeknight cooking — rice and beans, roasted vegetables, grilled anything.

1. Tahini-lemon sauce

Whisk tahini with lemon juice, a splash of water, a grated garlic clove, and salt until it loosens into a pourable sauce. Goes on roasted vegetables, grain bowls, or thinned further as a salad dressing.

2. Soy-honey glaze

Combine soy sauce, honey, a spoon of rice vinegar, and chili flakes. Works as a stir-fry sauce, a glaze for roasted vegetables in the last five minutes of cooking, or a dip.

3. Cumin-tomato base

Sauté onion and garlic, add crushed tomatoes, cumin, and smoked paprika, and simmer fifteen minutes. This is the base for a quick chili with the canned beans, or a sauce for rice and eggs.

A pantry isn't a list of ingredients. It's a list of things that talk to each other.

What's deliberately missing

No specialty vinegars, no single-use spice blends, no ingredient that shows up in exactly one recipe on this site. If you find yourself buying something for a single dish and never touching it again, that's usually a sign to swap it for something already on this list — most cuisines have a version of "acid, aromatic, fat, heat" that overlaps more than people expect.
