The Sunday Batch-Cook Plan

One two-hour session on Sunday turns into five weeknight dinners. The trick isn't cooking five separate meals — it's cooking three flexible components and recombining them all week.

15mActive prep
45mHands-off cook
5Dinners produced
2Dishes to wash

Most meal-prep advice fails for one reason: it asks you to cook the same finished dish five times, and by Wednesday you can't face another container of identical stir-fry. This plan is built differently. You cook three components — a grain, a protein, and a vegetable base — and each night you combine them with a different sauce and one fresh addition. Same prep session, five dinners that don't taste like leftovers.

The three components

Everything below is designed to cook in parallel: the grain and protein both go in the oven or on the stove unattended while you handle the vegetables by hand. Nothing here needs your full attention for more than fifteen minutes at a stretch.

Shopping list — serves 4, five dinners

  • 2 cups dry rice or farro
  • 2 lb chicken thighs, boneless
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt, black pepper
  • 2 lb mixed vegetables (broccoli, peppers, carrots)
  • 1 red onion
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 lemon
  • Soy sauce, for one night's sauce
  • Plain yogurt, for one night's sauce
  • Hot sauce of choice
  • Fresh herbs (cilantro or parsley), small bunch

1. The grain

Rinse two cups of rice or farro and cook according to package directions, but with one change: cook it in stock instead of water if you have it, and salt it properly. Under-salted grain is the single most common reason batch-cooked meals taste flat by Wednesday — you're not seasoning it again later, so it needs to carry flavor on its own. Spread the cooked grain on a sheet tray to cool before storing; piling it hot into a container turns it gummy.

2. The protein

Toss the chicken thighs with olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper. Roast at 425°F for 22–25 minutes on a sheet tray until the internal temperature hits 165°F. Thighs are far more forgiving than chicken breast here — they stay juicy even after four days of reheating, which is the actual point of choosing them for a batch-cook plan. Let the chicken rest for ten minutes, then slice or shred it.

3. The vegetables

Cut everything into roughly similar sizes so it cooks evenly. Roast half the vegetables plain, with just oil, salt, and pepper — these go into the more neutral-flavored dinners later in the week. Leave the other half raw or lightly blanched, so you have a crisp, fresh-tasting option for the nights you want contrast rather than more roasted flavor.

The five dinners

Same base, five distinct meals. The variable each night is the sauce and one small fresh addition — that's what keeps this from tasting repetitive.

  • Monday — Lemon-herb bowl. Grain, chicken, roasted vegetables, a squeeze of lemon, and chopped fresh herbs stirred through at the end.
  • Tuesday — Yogurt-garlic bowl. Mix plain yogurt with a grated garlic clove and a pinch of salt; spoon over the grain, chicken, and raw vegetables.
  • Wednesday — Quick soy-ginger stir-fry. Reheat chicken and vegetables in a hot pan with a splash of soy sauce for two minutes; serve over the grain.
  • Thursday — Hot sauce and crunch. Grain, chicken, raw vegetables, hot sauce to taste. The fastest of the five — under three minutes to plate.
  • Friday — Freezer backup. If you have a portion left over, this is the one you froze on Sunday. Defrost overnight in the fridge, reheat, done — no decisions required on a Friday.
The goal isn't five different recipes. It's one system flexible enough that Wednesday's dinner doesn't taste like Monday's.

Storage notes

Store the grain, protein, and roasted vegetables in separate containers, not pre-combined — this is what actually lets you make five different meals instead of one meal in five containers. Everything keeps for four days refrigerated. If you're prepping the Friday freezer backup, portion it before it ever goes in the fridge, and freeze it flat in a bag so it thaws quickly.

Adjusting the plan

Swap the protein for chickpeas or baked tofu to make the whole week vegetarian — the cook times and storage logic stay the same. Swap the grain for roasted potatoes if you want something lower-carb-adjacent-but-still-a-grain-vibe. The structure — one grain, one protein, one vegetable, five sauces — is the part worth keeping; the specific ingredients are just this week's version of it.
